This Table of Authorities comprises the cases, statutes, and legal precedents cited in the First Amended Complaint and supporting strategy documents within your sources.

### **I. Cases**
*   **Arizona v. Gant**, 556 U.S. 332 (2009): Establishes that a vehicle search incident to arrest is only lawful if the arrestee is within reaching distance of the passenger compartment or evidence of the offense might be found in the vehicle.
*   **Ashcroft v. Iqbal**, 556 U.S. 662 (2009): Defines the federal pleading standard requiring factual allegations that state a "plausible" claim for relief.
*   **Atwater v. City of Lago Vista**, 532 U.S. 318 (2001): Permits a warrantless custodial arrest for minor, fine-only traffic offenses, provided the offense is committed in the officer's presence or view.
*   **Bell Atl. Corp. v. Twombly**, 550 U.S. 544 (2007): Establishes the "plausibility" standard for surviving a Rule 12(b)(6) motion to dismiss.
*   **Brady v. Maryland**, 373 U.S. 83 (1963): Mandates the disclosure of material, exculpatory evidence by the prosecution.
*   **Buckley v. Fitzsimmons**, 509 U.S. 259 (1993): Holds that prosecutors lose absolute immunity when performing administrative or investigative functions.
*   **Burns v. Reed**, 500 U.S. 478 (1991): Establishes that prosecutors acting in an administrative capacity (such as giving legal advice to police) are not entitled to absolute immunity.
*   **Castellano v. Fragozo**, 352 F.3d 939 (5th Cir. 2003) (en banc): Outlines the requirements for a § 1983 malicious prosecution claim, which must be anchored to a Fourth Amendment seizure.
*   **Collins v. Virginia**, 138 S. Ct. 1663 (2018): Protects vehicles parked within the curtilage of a home from warrantless searches.
*   **County of Riverside v. McLaughlin**, 500 U.S. 44 (1991): Requires a prompt judicial determination of probable cause (generally within 48 hours) following a warrantless arrest.
*   **Dennis v. Sparks**, 449 U.S. 24 (1980): Clarifies that absolute immunity does not extend to conspiratorial acts involving state officials.
*   **Florida v. J.L.**, 529 U.S. 266 (2000): Established that reasonable suspicion must be measured by what an officer knew *before* a seizure, not through hindsight.
*   **Franks v. Delaware**, 438 U.S. 154 (1978): Allows a defendant to challenge a search warrant or arrest if the supporting affidavit contains deliberate lies or a reckless disregard for the truth.
*   **Gerstein v. Pugh**, 420 U.S. 103 (1975): Mandates a judicial determination of probable cause as a prerequisite to an extended pretrial detention following a warrantless arrest.
*   **Graham v. Connor**, 490 U.S. 386 (1989): Sets the "objective reasonableness" standard for evaluating Fourth Amendment excessive force claims based on the severity of the crime, threat level, and resistance.
*   **Griffin v. Breckenridge**, 403 U.S. 88 (1971): Establishes that 42 U.S.C. § 1985(3) reaches private conspiracies and state actors acting to deprive individuals of equal protection.
*   **Heck v. Humphrey**, 512 U.S. 477 (1994): Bars § 1983 claims for damages if a judgment in the plaintiff's favor would necessarily imply the invalidity of an outstanding criminal conviction or sentence.
*   **Hilliard v. Ferguson**, 30 F.3d 649 (5th Cir. 1994): Recognizes § 1983 conspiracy claims based on a "meeting of the minds" to violate constitutional rights without requiring class-based animus.
*   **Imbler v. Pachtman**, 424 U.S. 409 (1976): Establishes absolute prosecutorial immunity for acts "intimately associated with the judicial phase of the criminal process".
*   **Jauch v. Choctaw County**, 874 F.3d 425 (5th Cir. 2017): Holds that an 18-hour detention for a fine-only misdemeanor without a judicial determination of probable cause is unconstitutional.
*   **Lugar v. Edmondson Oil Co.**, 457 U.S. 922 (1982): Defines when private parties (like a towing company) act "under color of state law" and can be sued under § 1983.
*   **Monell v. Department of Social Services**, 436 U.S. 658 (1978): Establishes municipal liability under § 1983 when an official policy or custom is the "moving force" behind a constitutional violation.
*   **Pembaur v. City of Cincinnati**, 475 U.S. 469 (1986): Establishes that a single decision by a final policymaker can represent official municipal policy.
*   **Rodriguez v. United States**, 575 U.S. 348 (2015): Rules that a traffic stop becomes unconstitutional if prolonged beyond the time necessary to complete the stop's initial mission.
*   **Stump v. Sparkman**, 435 U.S. 349 (1978): Confirms that judges are immune from suit for judicial acts unless performed in the "clear absence of all jurisdiction".
*   **Terry v. Ohio**, 392 U.S. 1 (1968): Requires that a seizure be justified at its inception by specific and articulable facts known to the officer.
*   **Trezevant v. City of Tampa**, 741 F.2d 336 (11th Cir. 1984): Upheld a $25,000 award for a 23-minute unlawful detention, establishing a high compensatory baseline for deprivations of liberty.
*   **Whren v. United States**, 517 U.S. 806 (1996): Dictates that an officer's subjective intent is irrelevant as long as objective probable cause exists at the moment of seizure.
*   **Williams v. Wood**, 612 F.2d 982 (5th Cir. 1980): Establishes that court clerks lose quasi-judicial immunity when acting maliciously or outside the scope of their duties.
*   **Wong Sun v. United States**, 371 U.S. 471 (1963): Establishes the "fruit of the poisonous tree" doctrine, voiding evidence and downstream acts resulting from an unconstitutional seizure.
*   **Zinermon v. Burch**, 494 U.S. 113 (1990): Holds that an arbitrary deprivation of liberty without procedural safeguards violates the Due Process Clause.

### **II. Constitutions and Statutes**
*   **U.S. Constitution, Amendment IV**: Protection against unreasonable searches and seizures.
*   **U.S. Constitution, Amendment VI**: Rights of the accused, including the right to notice and to be present.
*   **U.S. Constitution, Amendment VIII**: Protection against cruel and unusual punishment and excessive fines.
*   **U.S. Constitution, Amendment XIV**: Protection of Due Process and Equal Protection rights.
*   **28 U.S.C. § 1331**: Federal Question Jurisdiction.
*   **28 U.S.C. § 1343(a)**: Civil Rights Jurisdiction.
*   **42 U.S.C. § 1983**: Civil action for deprivation of rights.
*   **42 U.S.C. § 1985(3)**: Conspiracy to interfere with civil rights.
*   **42 U.S.C. § 1986**: Action for neglect to prevent a conspiracy under § 1985.
*   **Texas Code of Criminal Procedure Art. 2.01**: Duties of District and County Attorneys to prosecute offenses.
*   **Texas Code of Criminal Procedure Art. 4.11 & 4.12**: Defines precinct and territorial jurisdiction for Justice Courts.
*   **Texas Code of Criminal Procedure Art. 14.01(b)**: Requires that a warrantless arrest for a misdemeanor be for an offense committed in the officer's "presence or within his view".
*   **Texas Code of Criminal Procedure Art. 39.14 (Michael Morton Act)**: Mandates the disclosure of all investigative police records and recordings.
*   **Texas Code of Criminal Procedure Art. 45A.251(d)**: Directs justice courts to credit a defendant for time served in jail toward a fine.
*   **Texas Government Code § 27.031**: Defines the jurisdiction of Justice of the Peace courts.
*   **Texas Government Code § 81.102**: Prohibits the unauthorized practice of law by non-attorneys.
*   **Texas Transportation Code § 502.407(b)**: Governing statute for expired registration, providing a remedial process for dismissal within 20 working days.
*   **Texas Transportation Code § 543.003 - 543.004**: Procedures for arrest and mandatory "notice to appear" for specific offenses like speeding or open containers.

### **III. Federal Rules of Civil Procedure (FRCP) and Evidence (FRE)**
*   **Fed. R. Civ. P. 10(c)**: Permits documents attached or embedded in a pleading to be treated as part of that pleading for all purposes.
*   **Fed. R. Civ. P. 11**: Establishes an attorney's duty to certify that factual contentions have evidentiary support to the best of their knowledge.
*   **Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(b)(6)**: Governs the dismissal of a case for failure to state a claim upon which relief can be granted.
*   **Fed. R. Civ. P. 38(b)**: Governing rule for requesting a jury trial.
*   **Fed. R. Evid. 201**: Allows a court to take "judicial notice" of indisputable facts or public records.
*   **Fed. R. Evid. 609**: Permits the use of prior criminal convictions for crimes of dishonesty to impeach witness credibility.
