# TIMELINE — This master timeline synthesizes the established physical, temporal, and documentary facts from the First Amended Complaint (FAC) and the official federal record in **Case No. 3:25-cv-00253**.

### **I. Pre-Incident: The Policymaker Foundation**
The litigation establishes a county-wide "custom of fabrication" anchored by the criminal records of final policymakers.
*   **Nov 18, 2019:** Judge Jason Sanders (JP1) pleads **Nolo Contendere** to **Tampering with Governmental Record** (Dkt. 38-1).
*   **June 25, 2021:** Sheriff Frank "Skipper" Osborne pleads **Guilty** to **Attempt to Commit Tamper with Governmental Record** (Dkt. 38).
*   **Feb 2022:** Plaintiff’s vehicle registration expires, becoming the eventual pretext for the seizure.

### **II. May 14, 2023: The "2220 Hardline" and Incident Day**
The entire case centers on a 120-second window where visual observation was a geometric impossibility.
*   **22:17:** Plaintiff leaves his residence (194 8th Street West, Markham) to go to a local store.
*   **22:20:** Deputy Raymond Gonzales activates emergency lights at the 4-way stop of Ave I and 6th Street.
*   **22:20:** Incident Report #2023-0477 (**Exhibit A**) logs both the "observation" of the offense and the "initiation" of lights at the identical minute, proving no pre-seizure verification occurred.
*   **22:22:** Plaintiff is in physical custody and handcuffed (Exhibits A and C).
*   **~22:23–22:30:** While Plaintiff is secured in transport, Deputy Gonzales and a female deputy conduct a warrantless exploratory search of the vehicle parked on **private curtilage** (507 Ave I).
*   **22:39:** Plaintiff is booked into the Matagorda County Jail in Bay City (7.6 miles away). This **17-minute window** from arrest to booking is a documented physical impossibility for a safe commute and intake processing.

### **III. May 15–16, 2023: The Fabrication Phase**
Official records prove that the criminal case was back-dated and manufactured after Plaintiff’s physical release.
*   **May 15 (~14:00):** Judge Sanders conducts a "picket window" magistration at the jail, telling Plaintiff, "We're going to get you outta here with time served".
*   **May 15 (16:23:54):** Plaintiff is officially released from custody (Resident Transaction Receipt).
*   **May 16:** The Register of Actions (**Exhibit E**) shows Case No. 1-23-TR-0306 is first **created and filed**—the day *after* release.
*   **May 16:** A "Nolo Contendere" plea and a conviction are entered on the docket *in absentia* for a man already out of custody; **Exhibit B** (Affidavit) remains **completely blank** regarding a judicial probable cause finding.

### **IV. June 2023: Suppression of Evidence and Tow Hearing**
Administrative barriers were utilized to blind the Plaintiff of exculpatory video nine days before trial.
*   **June 7:** Plaintiff retrieves his vehicle from Jackson Towing for $500 cash; the receipt identifies the vehicle as a "**White Chevrolet SLT**," adopting Gonzales's "ghost truck" fabrication.
*   **June 15 (08:34):** Records Clerk Michaela Riley emails (**Exhibit F**) providing exact minute runtimes (64:26 total) for bodycam/dashcam footage, quoting a cost of **$104.26**.
*   **June 16:** County Attorney Jennifer Chau’s office denies a fee waiver for the indigent Plaintiff.
*   **June 16:** Riley accepts $105 cash but returns it two hours later, claiming a sudden "technical malfunction" with the transfer station.
*   **June 20:** Riley prints the "DBD Arrest Report," showing judicial fields for Judgment and Disposition are **still blank** 36 days after release, contradicting the post-dated Exhibit E docket.
*   **June 22:** **Tow Hearing** (Cause No. 4-23-AH-0061) before Judge Mark Finlay (JP4). Finlay denies the motion to compel the suppressed video and rules the tow was legal based on Gonzales's perjured testimony that the car was in the "middle of the road".
