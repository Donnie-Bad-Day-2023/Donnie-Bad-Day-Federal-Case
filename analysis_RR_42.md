## KEY LEGAL AUTHORITIES CITED

### PRIMARY BASIS FOR ARREST FINDING:
**Atwater v. City of Lago Vista, 532 U.S. 318 (2001)**
- Supreme Court held: officer may arrest for minor offense without violating 4th Amendment
- "If an officer has probable cause to believe that an individual has committed even a very minor criminal offense in his presence, he may, without violating the Fourth Amendment, arrest the offender."

### TEXAS STATUTES REFERENCED:
- **§ 502.407** - Dismissal provision for expired registration (cited fn 4)
- **§ 543.004(c)** - Lists ONLY 3 offenses where notice to appear is MANDATORY: speeding, wireless communication device, open container
- **§ 543.003** - NOT discussed directly (this is Thompson's main statute)

### QUALIFIED IMMUNITY STANDARD:
- "Right must be clearly established"
- "Relevant precedent must place question beyond debate"
- Ashcroft v. al-Kidd, 563 U.S. 731 (2011)

### JUDICIAL IMMUNITY:
- Stump v. Sparkman, 435 U.S. 349 (1978)
- "Act in clear absence of all jurisdiction"

## THE R&R'S CRITICAL FAILINGS

### 1. ATWATER ARGUMENT NOT FULLY ADDRESSED
The Magistrate uses Atwater (2001) to justify the arrest. But:
- Atwater predates the current Texas Transportation Code scheme
- Thompson argues § 502.407 implicitly limits arrest authority
- R&R says "Thompson overlooks" § 543.004(c) but doesn't explain WHY § 502.407 doesn't limit arrest
- The argument that § 543.004(c) is EXCLUSIVE is assumed, not proven

### 2. § 543.003 NOT DISCUSSED
Thompson's primary argument is under § 543.003 which REQUIRES citation for fine-only offenses. The R&R never directly addresses this statute - it only cites § 543.004(c) to show expired registration isn't on the "mandatory notice" list.

**This is a gap:** § 543.004(c) says what IS required (notice to appear for 3 offenses). It doesn't say what is PROHIBITED. Thompson's argument is about what officer MUST do, not what is prohibited from arresting.

### 3. QUALIFIED IMMUNITY CONFLATION
The Magistrate says: "even if Thompson's theory is correct (it is not), his argument is so novel that Gonzales would be entitled to qualified immunity anyway."

**This is legally problematic:**
- It says the constitutional question doesn't matter because the law wasn't clear enough
- But the standard is whether RIGHT was clearly established - not whether the specific THEORY was previously rejected
- Thompson's theory is based on plain language of statutes that have existed for years

### 4. DUE PROCESS ARGUMENT NOT ADDRESSED
Thompson argues:
- He was never formally charged
- Never pled to the offense
- Never adjudicated on the merits
- Yet received "criminal punishment" (time served)

The R&R addresses judicial immunity but does NOT address whether due process requires formal charging. Judge Sanders "credited time served" - but if there was no valid charge, what was time served crediting against?

### 5. MONELL ANALYSIS ASSUMES NO VIOLATION
The Monell claim is dismissed because "Thompson cannot establish a constitutional violation." But this is circular - the violation claim IS the Monell basis. If the arrest was unlawful under state law, that may be the constitutional violation.

## RECOMMENDED OBJECTION POINTS

### Objection 1: R&R Failed to Conduct Independent Review (FRCP 72)
- The R&R adopts Defense MTD wholesale without independent analysis
- Fails to address § 543.003 argument
- Does not analyze why § 502.407 doesn't limit arrest authority

### Objection 2: Misapplication of Atwater v. Lago Vista
- Atwater is distinguishable - it predates Texas's current statutory scheme
- Atwater concerned whether arrest was constitutional under FOURTH AMENDMENT
- Thompson's argument is under TEXAS STATUTORY LAW requiring citation
- Federal courts should not interpret state law - but the R&R does exactly that

### Objection 3: Qualified Immunity Error
- Qualified immunity requires showing NO clearly established right
- The R&R concludes no constitutional violation EXISTS, not that the right wasn't clear
- These are different analyses
- If the statute clearly required citation, then arresting instead was clearly wrong

### Objection 4: Due Process Claim Ignored
- R&R does not address the due process argument about uncharged punishment
- Time served as "punishment" for something never formally charged raises serious due process issues
- Judge Sanders' magistration did not involve formal charging or plea

### Objection 5: Rooker-Feldman Misapplied
- Thompson's claims are about the FEDERAL constitutional violation from arrest
- Rooker-Feldman bars review of STATE COURT JUDGMENTS
- The arrest, not the tow hearing, is the constitutional violation at issue
- Review of the arrest does not require rejection of state court proceedings

### Objection 6: Judicial Immunity Does Not Resolve Constitutional Claim
- Judge Sanders' immunity does not resolve whether the arrest itself violated federal law
- The unconstitutional arrest preceded any state court involvement
- Federal claim against Gonzales is separate from claim against Sanders
