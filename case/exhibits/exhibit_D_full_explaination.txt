Exhibit D — Vehicle Inventory Report

Case: Donald Lynn Thompson Jr. v. Matagorda County et al.

Case No.: 3:25-cv-00253 — USDC S.D. Tex., Galveston Division

Document Summary

The vehicle inventory that is not inventoried, not signed, not authorized — a legal nullity. This document also

provides a fourth vehicle description ("2 door SLT") that is inconsistent with every prior description and with

the actual vehicle.

Four Fatal Defects

Defect 1 — Nothing inventoried: The "Extra Accessories on Vehicle" and "Accessories Removed Prior to

Inventory" sections are completely blank. Nothing was listed as inventoried.

Defect 2 — Not signed or dated: The deputy signature line and date fields are blank. No officer certified the

inventory.

Defect 3 — Wrong location: The vehicle is listed as seized/removed from 107 Ave I — not 507 Ave I where

the stop actually occurred. This is a different address entirely, further undermining the credibility of the official

record.

Defect 4 — Fourth vehicle description: The Year/Make/Model field reads "2004 Chrys Sebring 2-door

SLT." "SLT" is a Dodge trim designation, not a Chrysler designation, and has no application to a Sebring. This

is now the fourth distinct vehicle description across the official record:

1. Incident Report Assignment: "Chrysler, Sebring, 2-Door" (correct model)

2. Incident Report Summary: "said pickup" (impossible — Chrysler makes no Sebring pickup)

3. Sworn Affidavit: "2 DOOR SEDAN" (impossible model classification)

4. Vehicle Inventory: "2-door SLT" (wrong manufacturer's trim designation)

Strategic Significance

An unsigned, undated vehicle inventory with nothing inventoried and a wrong address is a legal nullity — it

cannot serve as documentation of a lawful impound. Under Arizona v. Gant, 556 U.S. 332 (2009), and Collins v.

Virginia, 138 S. Ct. 1663 (2018), a warrantless search and seizure of a vehicle on private property requires

specific legal justification that this document cannot provide.

The wrong address (107 vs. 507 Ave I) is not a clerical error — it is evidence that the inventory was not

prepared at the scene and was fabricated after the fact.
