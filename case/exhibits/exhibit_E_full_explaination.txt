Document Summary

Matagorda County Register of Actions for Case No. 1-23-TR-0306, pulled from the county's public website.

This document demonstrates a post-dated conviction entered in absentia on May 16, 2023 — the day after

Plaintiff's physical release from custody — without Plaintiff's knowledge, presence, or participation.

Key Evidentiary Facts

The post-dated docket: Every event in this Register of Actions — the plea, the judgment, the new case filing,

the incident report, the booking sheet, the order of commitment, the release report — is dated 05/16/2023.

Plaintiff was arrested on 5/14/2023, held 18 hours and 1 minute, and released on 5/15/2023 at 16:23. The case

was created the day after release.

No prosecution existed at the time of release: When Judge Sanders appeared at the jail on 5/15/2023 and

told Plaintiff "we're going to give you time served and get you out of here," there was no case number, no

information filed, no citation issued, no charging instrument. The Register of Actions proves this — everything

is dated 5/16.

The fabricated conviction: The disposition shows "Nolo Contendere" plea and "Convicted" judgment — both

entered on 05/16/2023, the day after release, without Plaintiff present, without Plaintiff's knowledge, and

without Plaintiff entering any plea.

This document is the core proof that the "conviction" was fabricated. Judge Sanders had no jurisdiction to

impose a sentence on 5/15/2023 because:

1. The case was not filed until 5/16/2023 — the day after the "sentence."

2. The offense occurred in JP4's precinct (Markham), not JP1's precinct.

3. No information, citation, or indictment had been filed.

4. No plea was entered by Plaintiff — the Nolo Contendere plea was entered in absentia.

5. The probable cause signature on the Sworn Affidavit (Exhibit B) was never signed.

This is the "phantom charge" referenced throughout the amended complaint. Sanders imposed time served

for a crime the State of Texas had not yet formally brought. The entire docket was then created retroactively

the following day.

Controlling authority: Gerstein v. Pugh, 420 U.S. 103 (1975) (prompt judicial determination of probable cause

required); Stump v. Sparkman, 435 U.S. 349 (1978) (judicial immunity fails for acts outside jurisdiction).
