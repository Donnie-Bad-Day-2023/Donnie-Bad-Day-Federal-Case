Exhibit F — Riley Email: Footage Suppression

Evidence

Case: Donald Lynn Thompson Jr. v. Matagorda County et al.

Case No.: 3:25-cv-00253 — USDC S.D. Tex., Galveston Division

Document Summary

Email from Defendant Michaela Riley (Records/ID Clerk, Matagorda County Sheriff's Office) dated June 15,

2023, establishing the specific timestamps of body and dash camera footage that was intact nine days before

the tow hearing — and subsequently suppressed.

Key Evidentiary Facts

The footage existed and was intact: Riley's email confirms footage existed for 3 officers and 1 dashcam as of

June 15, 2023 — nine days before the June 22, 2023 tow hearing. She provided specific minute counts for

each recording.

The footage timestamps:

Body cam footage per video: 14:11 + 14:50 + 14:11 + 21:14 minutes

Total minutes: 64.26 minutes across 4 recordings

Cost quoted: $104.26 ($10 per recording + $1 per minute)

"Technical issues" was false: Riley returned Plaintiff's $105.00 payment on June 20, 2023 — two days before

the hearing — claiming "technical issues" with the system prevented her from producing the recordings. But

she had already confirmed the footage existed and provided exact minute counts on June 15. You cannot

quote exact minute counts for footage that has "technical issues." The footage was suppressed, not

unavailable.

The financial barrier: Riley's demand for $104.26 was an unauthorized, arbitrary financial barrier. Plaintiff's

IFP (in forma pauperis) status had already been granted by JP4 Judge Finlay. The fee demand was not waived

despite documented indigency. Email Content

"ok thank you, I will be in Monday to make arrangements to pick up the information." What Happened Next

On approximately June 20, 2023 — two days before the hearing — Riley returned Plaintiff's $105.00 payment

and stated the recordings could not be produced due to "technical issues." No explanation was provided for

why specific minute counts had been quoted five days earlier if the system was experiencing technical issues.

The footage was not produced before or during the June 22, 2023 tow hearing. Judge Finlay denied Virginia

Thompson's oral motion to compel production and continue the hearing.

Strategic Significance

This email is the factual anchor for Count VI (Suppression of Exculpatory Evidence) against Defendant Riley. It

proves:

1. The footage existed as of June 15, 2023 with exact minute counts.

2. The footage was not produced before the June 22 tow hearing.

3. The "technical issues" claim was false — you cannot quote exact footage timestamps for recordings that

are technically inaccessible.

4. The financial barrier was unauthorized given Plaintiff's IFP status.

The five specific things the footage would have shown (per ¶57 of the First Amended Complaint): vehicle

location in Tina Phillips's driveway; proximity duration between vehicles; absence of any plate query; Plaintiff's

non-resistant demeanor; and the warrantless search of the secured vehicle.

Controlling authority: Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83 (1963); Tex. Code Crim. Proc. Ann. art. 39.14 (Michael

Morton Act); Burns v. Reed, 500 U.S. 478 (1991) (administrative role = no prosecutorial immunity).
