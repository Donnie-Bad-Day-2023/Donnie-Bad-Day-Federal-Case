# R&R vs. MTD — WORDING COMPARISON
## Demonstrating the R&R's wholesale adoption of defense arguments

---

## EXAMPLE 1: Section 543.004(c) "Exhaustive List" Argument

### Defense MTD (Dkt 29, p. 3):
> "Thompson overlooks that, under Texas law, speeding, use of a wireless communication device, or a violation of the open container law 'are the only offenses for which issuance of a written notice to appear is mandatory.' Tex. Transp. Code § 543.004(c) (emphasis added). Whatever prohibition against arrest Thompson believes is implied by § 502.407 cannot stand in the face of the plain language of § 543.004, which articulates exactly three offenses for which officers may not make an arrest, none of which is an expired registration."

### Magistrate R&R (Dkt 42, p. 4):
> "Thompson overlooks that, under Texas law, speeding, use of a wireless communication device, or a violation of the open container law 'are the only offenses for which issuance of a written notice to appear is mandatory.' Tex. Transp. Code § 543.004(c) (emphasis added). Whatever prohibition against arrest Thompson believes is implied by § 502.407 cannot stand in the face of the plain language of § 543.004, which articulates exactly three offenses for which officers may not make an arrest, none of which is an expired registration."

**Assessment:** The R&R reproduced the MTD's argument verbatim — including the phrase "Whatever prohibition against arrest Thompson believes is implied by § 502.407 cannot stand" — without independently analyzing whether that argument was correct. The R&R did not address § 502.407's remedial scheme.

## EXAMPLE 2: Atwater as Controlling Authority

### Defense MTD (Dkt 29, p. 5):
> "If an officer has probable cause to believe that an individual has committed even a very minor criminal offense in his presence, he may, without violating the Fourth Amendment, arrest the offender."

### Magistrate R&R (Dkt 42, p. 5):
> "If an officer has probable cause to believe that an individual has committed even a very minor criminal offense in his presence, he may, without violating the Fourth Amendment, arrest the offender."

**Assessment:** Same verbatim quote from *Atwater v. City of Lago Vista*, 532 U.S. 318, 354 (2001). The R&R did not address *Virginia v. Moore*, 553 U.S. 164 (2008), which is the controlling precedent on whether state law violations give rise to Fourth Amendment claims — despite the fact that Plaintiff's Opposition raised *Moore*.

## EXAMPLE 3: Qualified Immunity "Novelty" Argument

### Defense MTD (Dkt 29, p. 6):
> "Even if the Court were inclined to accept Thompson's novel theory, the individual defendants are entitled to qualified immunity because the right at issue was not clearly established."

### Magistrate R&R (Dkt 42, p. 5):
> "even if Thompson's theory is correct (it is not), his argument is so novel that Gonzales would be entitled to qualified immunity anyway."

**Assessment:** The R&R amplified the MTD's argument by stating "it is not" — a characterization that goes beyond what the MTD itself said. The circular reasoning (assumes no constitutional violation occurred, then concludes QI applies) was not corrected.

## EXAMPLE 4: Monell Dismissal (Circular Reasoning)

### Defense MTD (Dkt 29, p. 9):
> "As for Matagorda County, Thompson cannot establish a constitutional violation that would give rise to Monell liability. Gonzales acted lawfully when he arrested Thompson for an expired registration."

### Magistrate R&R (Dkt 42, p. 6):
> "As for Matagorda County, Thompson cannot establish a constitutional violation that would give rise to Monell liability. Gonzales acted lawfully when he arrested Thompson for an expired registration."

**Assessment:** Identical phrasing. The R&R did not independently assess whether the arrest was lawful — it assumed the conclusion from the MTD and used that assumption to dismiss Monell.

## EXAMPLE 5: Sua Sponte Dismissal of Jackson Towing

### Defense MTD: **Does not mention Jackson Towing at all.**

### Magistrate R&R (Dkt 42, p. 7-8):
> "Finally, the Court notes that there are only two references to Jackson in the complaint, and the claims against Jackson appear to be wholly derivative of the claims against the other Defendants. The Court recommends dismissing the claims against Jackson Towing Services sua sponte before the court enters final judgment."

**Assessment:** The R&R sua sponte recommended dismissing a defendant that the defense itself did not move to dismiss. This was done without:
- Any motion filed by defense
- A show-cause order
- Notice to Plaintiff
- Opportunity to amend

This violates due process under *Carroll v. Fort James Corp.*, 470 F.3d 1171, 1177 (5th Cir. 2006).
