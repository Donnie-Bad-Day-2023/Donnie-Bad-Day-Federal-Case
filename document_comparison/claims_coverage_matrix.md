## THE Rooker-Feldman ISSUE

The R&R invokes Rooker-Feldman in **Footnote 3** to bar Plaintiff's challenges to the state court tow hearing. This is error because:

1. Plaintiff is not asking this Court to reverse any state court judgment
2. Plaintiff's claims are independent federal § 1983 claims about constitutional violations
3. The Supreme Court in *Exxon Mobil* preserved the distinction between direct appellate review and independent federal claims
4. *Dennis v. Spurs*, 875 F.3d 865, 869 (5th Cir. 2017): Rooker-Feldman does not bar § 1983 claims challenging defendants' conduct, not the state court judgment

---

## CRITICAL FAILURE: THE R&R NEVER ANALYZES § 502.407

The core of Plaintiff's case is that Texas Transportation Code § 502.407 places expired registration in **Subtitle A** (General Provisions) with a specific remedial scheme:

- **20 working days** to cure the defect
- **$20 maximum** penalty
- **Mandatory dismissal** upon proof of valid registration
- **No incarceration**, no criminal conviction

This is fundamentally incompatible with custodial arrest. The Legislature designated this as an administrative matter, not a criminal one.

**The R&R does not mention § 502.407 by number, does not discuss Subtitle A, and does not analyze whether the remedial scheme limits arrest authority.**

Compare this to the MTD, which also does not analyze § 502.407 — the R&R simply adopted the MTD's framework without filling in the analytical gap that the MTD itself left.
