## I. INTRODUCTION

The Magistrate's Report and Recommendation ("R&R") suffers from a fundamental structural flaw: rather than conducting the independent de novo review required by 28 U.S.C. § 636(b)(1) and Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 72(b), the R&R functions as an advocate's brief for the defense. It reproduces the defense's arguments verbatim, declines to address the vast majority of Plaintiff's claims, and reaches conclusions that the Fifth Circuit has explicitly cautioned against at the motion-to-dismiss stage.

Most critically, the R&R fails to address—let alone rebut—Plaintiff's primary legal argument: that Texas Transportation Code § 502.407's specific remedial framework for expired vehicle registration operates as a limitation on general arrest authority under § 543.003. The R&R mentions § 502.407 only in a footnote, then proceeds to analyze only § 543.004—the statute Defendants cited. This is legal error requiring de novo review.

The objections herein are timely filed pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 636(b)(1) and Fed. R. Civ. P. 72(b).

## II. LEGAL STANDARD

When a party objects to a magistrate judge's recommendation, the district court must conduct a "de novo review of any portion of the magistrate judge's disposition that has been properly objected to." 28 U.S.C. § 636(b)(1)(C). The court may adopt, modify, or reject the magistrate judge's recommendation, but it may not simply defer to the magistrate judge's analysis when valid objections have been raised.

This standard exists precisely because magistrate judges, while performing valuable functions, are not infallible—and the adversarial process depends on the understanding that a neutral judicial officer will actually engage with both sides' arguments. When a magistrate judge merely reproduces one party's briefing without engaging with the other side's core arguments, the conditions for de novo review are present.

## III. OBJECTION NO. 1: THE R&R FAILED TO ADDRESS PLAINTIFF'S CENTRAL STATUTORY ARGUMENT REGARDING TEXAS TRANSPORTATION CODE § 502.407

### A. The Argument Was Raised and Preserved

In Plaintiff's Opposition (Dkt. 33), Section III.A.2, Plaintiff explicitly argued:

> "The Texas Legislature enacted § 502.407 to address the precise situation at issue in this case — operation of a vehicle with expired registration. The statute provides that a justice of the peace 'may dismiss a charge of driving with an expired motor vehicle registration' upon proof of remedial compliance, with a maximum financial consequence of $20. This statutory framework demonstrates that the Legislature did not intend for expired registration to be addressed through custodial arrest, jail detention, and criminal disposition."

Plaintiff further argued that specific statutory provisions govern over general arrest authority, citing *Jarecki v. G.D. Searle & Co.*, 367 U.S. 303, 307 (1961), and *RadLAX Gateway Hotel, LLC v. Amalgamated Bank*, 566 U.S. 639, 645 (2012).

### B. The R&R Does Not Address This Argument

The R&R does not mention § 502.407 in the body of its analysis. The only mention of § 502.407 appears in Footnote 4, which reads:

> "Under § 502.407 a justice of the peace may 'dismiss a charge of driving with an expired motor vehicle registration if the defendant . . . remedies the defect not later than the 20th working day after the date of the offense or before the defendant's first court appearance date, whichever is later.' Tex. Transp. Code § 502.407(b)(1)(A)."

This footnote is introduced in the context of dismissing Plaintiff's argument as "creative" but "overlook[ing]" § 543.004. But § 543.004 is the statute the *defense* cited—not the statute Plaintiff argued controls.

The R&R entirely fails to engage with Plaintiff's argument that § 502.407's specific remedial framework operates as a limitation on arrest authority for this offense. This is reversible error because:

1. **The argument is legally substantial**: The Supreme Court has long held that specific statutory provisions govern over general ones. If the Legislature created a specific remedial process for expired registration that requires dismissal upon compliance, with a maximum $20 consequence, this is evidence that custodial arrest was not the intended enforcement mechanism.

2. **The argument was not addressed**: A magistrate judge cannot conduct de novo review of arguments that are simply omitted from the R&R.

3. **The factual premise matters**: Plaintiff alleged he was never given notice of the § 502.407 remedy, never advised of his right to cure, and was never given an opportunity to comply. These facts, accepted as true, support the inference that the arrest was not only unconstitutional but contrary to the Legislature's intent.

### C. Request for Relief

Plaintiff respectfully requests that the Court conduct de novo review of Plaintiff's § 502.407 argument, which is the central legal theory of Count I, and that the Court reject the R&R's recommendation to dismiss Count I.

## IV. OBJECTION NO. 2: THE R&R'S ATWATER ANALYSIS IS LEGALLY ERRONEOUS

### A. The R&R's Reliance on Atwater Is Misplaced

The R&R's central legal premise is:

> "There is ample authority, from the United States Supreme Court down to the lowest courts, stating that: 'If an officer has probable cause to believe that an individual has committed even a very minor criminal offense in his presence, he may, without violating the Fourth Amendment, arrest the offender.' Atwater v. City of Lago Vista, 532 U.S. 318, 354 (2001)."

This is an incomplete statement of the holding in Atwater. Atwater itself recognized that "the business of the judiciary is to inquire into the sufficiency of the protestations of the officer." *Id.* at 354-55 n.17. Critically, Atwater addressed an arrest for a seatbelt violation under Texas law—and the Supreme Court was required to determine whether Texas law authorized arrest for that particular offense.

The R&R cites Atwater for the proposition that any officer may arrest for any minor offense. This misreads Atwater. The actual holding is: an arrest is reasonable under the Fourth Amendment only if the arrest is lawful under state law. If Texas law specifically provides that expired registration is to be addressed through citation and dismissal upon compliance, then an arrest for that offense may violate the Fourth Amendment—not because the federal constitution prohibits it categorically, but because the arrest was unlawful under state law.

### B. The R&R Conflates the Stop with the Arrest

The R&R states: "Thompson concedes in his complaint that his registration was expired." But the issue is not whether the stop was valid. The issue is whether the *custodial arrest* was lawful. The R&R never separates these distinct constitutional inquiries:

1. **Traffic stop (investigatory seizure)**: Requires reasonable suspicion of a traffic violation. Plaintiff does not contest this.

2. **Custodial arrest**: Requires probable cause AND statutory authority to take the person into custody. This is what is at issue.

The R&R conflates these two distinct seizures, treating the existence of probable cause for the stop as automatically validating the arrest. Fifth Circuit precedent requires separate analysis. *See Whren v. United States*, 517 U.S. 806, 810 (1996) (proportionality requirement); *Graham v. Connor*, 490 U.S. 386, 396 (1989) (reasonableness analysis requires attention to the "totality of the circumstances").

### C. Request for Relief

Plaintiff respectfully requests that the Court conduct de novo review of the legal question of whether Texas law authorized custodial arrest for expired registration under the specific remedial framework of § 502.407, separate and apart from whether probable cause existed for the stop.

## V. OBJECTION NO. 3: THE R&R ERRED IN ITS QUALIFIED IMMUNITY ANALYSIS

### A. The "Novelty" Argument Is Wrong

The R&R states:

> "Thompson bears a heavy burden because a right is clearly established only if relevant precedent has 'placed the statutory or constitutional question beyond debate.' Ashcroft v. al-Kidd, 563 U.S. 731, 741 (2011). Accordingly, even if Thompson's theory is correct (it is not), his argument is so novel that Gonzales would be entitled to qualified immunity anyway."

This reasoning is flawed for several reasons:

1. **The legal argument is not novel**: Plaintiff's argument is that § 502.407 creates a remedial framework that limits arrest authority for expired registration. This is a straightforward application of the principle that specific statutes govern over general ones. The text of § 502.407 has existed for decades. The argument is not that a "new right" was clearly established—it's that the statutory text itself governs the officer's authority.

2. **Qualified immunity is fact-specific**: The inquiry is whether a reasonable officer in Gonzales's position would have understood that custodial arrest was unlawful. If § 502.407 on its face establishes a remedial, non-custodial process for expired registration, a reasonable officer would be on notice that custodial arrest is not the proper response.

3. **The R&R assumes its conclusion**: The R&R says "even if Thompson's theory is correct (it is not)"—but this is precisely the question that must be decided. The R&R cannot assume the answer to the central legal question in the qualified immunity analysis.

### B. Request for Relief

Plaintiff respectfully requests that the Court conduct de novo review of the qualified immunity analysis and reject the R&R's conclusion that qualified immunity defeats Count I at this stage.

## VI. OBJECTION NO. 4: THE R&R FAILED TO CONDUCT INDEPENDENT REVIEW OF THE JUDICIAL IMMUNITY ARGUMENTS

### A. The R&R Adopted Defendants' Arguments Without Independent Analysis

The R&R devotes approximately one page to judicial immunity, relying primarily on *Stump v. Sparkman*, 435 U.S. 349 (1978), and Texas Code of Criminal Procedure Article 454.251(d). But Plaintiff raised specific arguments in his Opposition (Section III.E) that the R&R failed to address:

1. **Judge Sanders acted in the clear absence of jurisdiction**: Plaintiff argued that Judge Sanders conducted a magistration and imposed "time served" without a formal charge, without a plea, without a finding of guilt, and without advising Plaintiff of his rights. This is not merely error—it is a fundamental jurisdictional defect. The R&R responds only that Article 454.251(d) authorized Judge Sanders to credit time served—but Article 454.251(d) applies to criminal cases where there has been a valid charge, a valid plea, and a valid finding of guilt. Judge Sanders skipped every prerequisite.

2. **The R&R does not address the § 502.407 connection**: Plaintiff argued that Judge Sanders's imposition of "time served" actively eliminated Plaintiff's statutory right to dismissal under § 502.407(b). If the charge had been properly filed and Plaintiff had been given notice, he could have cured the defect within 20 working days and obtained dismissal. Judge Sanders's "time served" disposition foreclosed this remedy. The R&R does not address this argument.

### B. Request for Relief

Plaintiff respectfully requests that the Court conduct de novo review of the judicial immunity issue, with specific attention to whether Judge Sanders acted in the "clear absence of all jurisdiction" by imposing criminal punishment for an administrative offense that was never properly charged, pled, or adjudicated.

## VIII. OBJECTION NO. 6: THE R&R'S MONELL ANALYSIS IS CIRCULAR

### A. The R&R Defeats Monell Liability by Assuming Its Central Conclusion

The R&R states:

> "As for Matagorda County, Thompson cannot establish a constitutional violation that would give rise to Monell liability. Gonzales acted lawfully when he arrested Thompson for an expired registration; Sanders acted lawfully when he credited Thompson's time served toward the fine for an expired registration; and Chau did nothing more than appear at a tow hearing on the County's behalf."

This is circular reasoning. The R&R defeats Monell liability by first concluding that the arrest was lawful, the magistration was lawful, and Chau did nothing wrong. But these are precisely the conclusions that Plaintiff disputed and that form the basis of his claims.

If the Court determines, on de novo review, that the arrest was unlawful under § 502.407, then Monell liability must be reconsidered. The R&R cannot use its own (disputed) legal conclusions to defeat the Monell claim.

### B. Request for Relief

Plaintiff respectfully requests that the Court conduct de novo review of the Monell analysis and reconsider whether, under a correct reading of Texas law, Plaintiff has stated a claim for municipal liability under Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 (1978).

## IX. OBJECTION NO. 7: THE R&R'S INVOCATION OF ROOKER-FELDMAN IS ERROR

### A. Rooker-Feldman Does Not Apply

The R&R states in Footnote 5:

> "To the extent that Thompson seeks to relitigate the proceedings before either Judge Sanders or Judge Finlay, the Rooker-Feldman doctrine bars federal courts from exercising jurisdiction over 'cases brought by state-court losers complaining of injuries caused by state-court judgments rendered before the district court proceedings commenced and inviting district court review and rejection of those judgments.' Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Saudi Basic Indus. Corp., 544 U.S. 280, 284 (2005)."

This is incorrect. Rooker-Feldman applies when a plaintiff seeks direct appellate review of a state court judgment in federal district court. *See Lied Rapid Transit v. Detroit & Windsor Ferry Co.*, 419 U.S. 555 (1975). It does not apply to independent federal claims under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 alleging that constitutional violations occurred in connection with state court proceedings.

Plaintiff is not asking this Court to review Judge Sanders's or Judge Finlay's decisions as an appellate court. Plaintiff is bringing independent federal claims under § 1983 for constitutional violations that occurred during those proceedings. The Supreme Court has expressly preserved this distinction:

> "A § 1983 action is a 'parallel' suit, not an appeal, when the plaintiff is not asking the district court to review and reverse the state court judgment, but to redress injuries caused by the state actors' unconstitutional conduct." *Argo v. Blue Cross & Blue Shield of Md.*, 339 F.3d 1089, 1096 (9th Cir. 2003).

### B. Request for Relief

Plaintiff respectfully requests that the Court reject the R&R's Rooker-Feldman analysis and clarify that Plaintiff's claims are independent federal claims under § 1983, not appeals of state court proceedings.

## X. OBJECTION NO. 8: THE R&R'S SUA SPONTE DISMISSAL OF JACKSON TOWING VIOLATES DUE PROCESS

### A. Plaintiff Was Not Given Notice or Opportunity to Respond

The R&R recommends sua sponte dismissing Plaintiff's claims against Jackson Towing Services "before the court enters final judgment." (Dkt. 42 at 7-8). However:

1. **No motion to dismiss was filed regarding Jackson**: The defense's Motion to Dismiss (Dkt. 29) addressed only the Matagorda County Defendants. Jackson was not mentioned in the MTD.

2. **No sua sponte show-cause order was issued**: The Court did not issue an order to show cause why Jackson should not be dismissed.

3. **Plaintiff had no opportunity to respond**: Plaintiff was not given notice that sua sponte dismissal was being considered and had no opportunity to file a response or amend his complaint to allege additional facts against Jackson.

The Due Process Clause requires that a party receive notice and an opportunity to be heard before a court dismisses a claim. *Mathews v. Eldridge*, 424 U.S. 319 (1976). A sua sponte dismissal without prior notice and opportunity to amend violates this fundamental requirement.

### B. Request for Relief

Plaintiff respectfully requests that any dismissal of Jackson Towing be with leave to amend, after appropriate notice, so that Plaintiff may allege additional factual detail to support his claims against Jackson.
