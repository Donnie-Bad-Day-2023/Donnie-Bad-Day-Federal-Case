# Legal Claims — § 1983 — 3:25-cv-00253

## Claim I: Fourth Amendment — Unlawful Seizure (Traffic Stop)
- **Constitutional Basis:** Fourth Amendment / Fourteenth Amendment (Terry v. Ohio; Article 14.01(b) TCCP)
- **Elements:**
  1. Plaintiff was seized (traffic stop = seizure)
  2. No reasonable suspicion of criminal activity existed prior to the stop
  3. Deputy had no lawful basis to initiate the stop
- **Defendants:** R. Gonzales (Deputy Raymond Gonzales), Doe Deputies
- **Key Facts:**
  - Deputy activated lights at 22:20 — simultaneous with observation of the vehicle
  - **Observation ≠ Verification:** The 22:20 observation is the reason-to-stop, not proof of the violation. Texas law requires VERIFICATION of the alleged violation BEFORE probable cause exists.
  - **No verification interval:** There was no time between 22:20 and 22:22 for any plate check, registration check, or confirmation the sticker was still expired. The vehicle could have been registered in the 15 months since the alleged February 2022 expiration.
  - Plaintiff's vehicle was traveling lawfully; no speeding, no erratic driving, no evasive maneuvers
  - Report does NOT articulate HOW the deputy observed the expired sticker at night, 90° angle, sticker on far side of vehicle, no street lights
  - **Four vehicle descriptions across documents prove no MDT query was run:**
    1. Assignment: "Chrysler Sebring 2-Door" (correct)
    2. Summary: "said pickup" (impossible — Chrysler never made a Sebring pickup)
    3. Affidavit: "2 DOOR SEDAN" (impossible model)
    4. Tow Inventory: "2 door SLT" (Dodge trim designation, impossible)
  - This is the threshold issue: if the stop was unlawful, everything that followed is fruit of the poisonous tree

## Claim II: Fourth Amendment — Unlawful Custodial Arrest
- **Constitutional Basis:** Fourth Amendment / Fourteenth Amendment; Texas C.C.P. Art. 14.01(b), 14.06(b)
- **Elements:**
  1. Plaintiff was arrested (seized and taken into custody)
  2. No probable cause existed for the arrest
  3. No warrant, no refusal to sign citation, no intoxication, no outstanding warrant — none of the Art. 14.01(b) exceptions were present
- **Defendants:** R. Gonzales, Doe Deputies
- **Key Facts:**
  - 502.407 is a Class C misdemeanor — § 543.003 TCCP mandates citation and release, NOT custodial arrest
  - Plaintiff was physically yanked from his 2-door convertible; door opened from outside (broken interior handle)
  - No citation offered; no opportunity to correct registration within 20-day remedial window
  - Arrest effectuated at 22:22 — approximately 2 minutes from lights activation; no investigation occurred
  - Bond set at **$0.00** (booking sheet) — Class C with no aggravating factors
  - **Verification-before-custody rule violated:** No plate check, no registration status check, no confirmation the sticker was still expired before custody was taken

## Claim III: Fourth Amendment — Unlawful Warrantless Search
- **Constitutional Basis:** Fourth Amendment; Arizona v. Gant; Collins v. Virginia
- **Elements:**
  1. A search of plaintiff's vehicle occurred
  2. No warrant, no consent, no applicable exception
- **Defendants:** R. Gonzales, J. Wamget, Doe Deputies
- **Key Facts:**
  - Vehicle was on private residential driveway (Tina Phillips's property), not a public roadway
  - Plaintiff was secured in the transport vehicle — Gant exception does not apply
  - Collins v. Virginia: warrantless seizure of vehicle on private curtilage requires specific legal justification
  - No consent given; vehicle searched with flashlights while plaintiff cuffed in patrol vehicle
  - Inventory form (Exhibit D) is unsigned, undated, wrong address (107 vs. 507 Ave I) — legal nullity
