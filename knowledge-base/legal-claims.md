# Legal Claims — § 1983 — 3:25-cv-00253

## Claim 1: Unlawful Arrest
- **Constitutional Basis:** Fourth Amendment / Fourteenth Amendment
- **Elements:** (1) defendant seized plaintiff; (2) without probable cause
- **Defendants:** Mark Finlay, C. Sanders, R. Gonzales, J. Riley, A. Chau, Doe Deputies
- **Evidence:** arrest report, bodycam footage (if available), witness statements

## Claim 2: Fabricated Prosecution
- **Constitutional Basis:** Fourteenth Amendment due process (freedom of liberty)
- **Elements:** (1) defendant(s) created/provided false evidence; (2) that evidence was used to prosecute plaintiff; (3) plaintiff was convicted/charged and suffered harm
- **Defendants:** Mark Finlay, C. Sanders, R. Gonzales, J. Riley, A. Chau, Doe Deputies
- **Evidence:** police reports, bodycam, witness statements, charging instrument

## Claim 3: Brady Violations
- **Constitutional Basis:** Fourteenth Amendment due process (Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83 (1963))
- **Elements:** (1) evidence is favorable (exculpatory or impeachment); (2) defendant suppressed it; (3) prejudice resulted
- **Evidence:** withheld police reports, withheld bodycam footage, any deals with witnesses
- **See also:** `brady-evidence.md`

## Claim 4: Monell Municipal Liability
- **Constitutional Basis:** 42 U.S.C. § 1983; Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 (1978)
- **Theory:** Matagorda County's policy, custom, or practice caused constitutional violations
- **Sub-theories:**
  - Failure to train peace officers on lawful arrest and Brady obligations
  - Ratification of unlawful conduct by supervisory defendants
  - Express policy or widespread custom of fabricating evidence
- **Defendants:** Matagorda County
- **See also:** `monell.md`
