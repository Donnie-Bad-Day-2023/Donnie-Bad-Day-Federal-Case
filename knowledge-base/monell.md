# Monell — Municipal Liability — 3:25-cv-00253

## Legal Standard
Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 (1978): A municipality is liable under § 1983 when an official policy, custom, or practice causes a constitutional violation.

## Theories of Liability Against Matagorda County

### Theory 1: Express Policy — Unlawful Arrest for Subtitle A Offenses
- Matagorda County Sheriff's Office maintains a policy of effectuating full custodial arrests for expired registration (502.407) — a Class C misdemeanor — without offering a citation, without verifying registration, and without any of the Article 14.01(b) exceptions being present
- This policy bypasses the statutory remedial function (20-day correction window) and the citation-and-release mandate of Texas TCCP § 543.003
- *Pending: confirm whether this is a written policy or express direction*

### Theory 2: Widespread Custom — Fabricated Reasonable Suspicion
- Persistent, widespread practice of claiming to have "observed expired registration" as a pretext for traffic stops and custodial arrests, without any articulated factual basis for how the observation was made
- Deputy Gonzales' incident report is representative: no explanation of how he saw the sticker at night, from a 90° angle, with the sticker on the far side of the vehicle turning away from him
- This custom is so prevalent it has become effectively policy
- *Pending: identify other incidents or complaints involving the same custom*

### Theory 3: Failure to Train
- Matagorda County failed to adequately train its peace officers on:
  - **Subtitle A vs. Subtitle C distinctions** under the Texas Transportation Code — Class C misdemeanors under Subtitle A (like 502.407) require citation and release, NOT custodial arrest
  - **Brady obligations** — duty to preserve and disclose bodycam/dashcam footage; failure to maintain recording systems
  - **Probable cause articulation** — officers must explain in their reports how they observed the specific violation
  - **Fourth Amendment seizure standards** — reasonable suspicion required before any traffic stop
- The failure to train reflects deliberate indifference to constitutional rights
- *Pending: obtain training records, any prior complaints or incidents involving the same failures*

### Theory 4: Ratification of Unconstitutional Conduct
- Sheriff's office supervisors and county legal counsel (Jennifer Chau) were aware of the unlawful stop, the false arrest, and the withheld bodycam footage
- They participated in the tow hearing and ratified the conduct by:
  - County Attorney Chau objecting to plaintiff's motion to compel bodycam footage
  - Judge Finlay denying the motion and accepting Deputy Gonzales' false testimony without evidence
  - No disciplinary action, no internal review, no correction of the false narrative
- *Pending: identify the final policymaker who had authority to correct this conduct and failed to do so*

## Key Elements to Prove
1. Plaintiff suffered a constitutional injury (unlawful stop, false arrest, fabricated prosecution, Brady violation, excessive force, unlawful detention, excessive fine)
2. The injury was caused by an official policy/custom/practice of Matagorda County
3. The policy/custom/practice reflects deliberate indifference to plaintiff's rights

## Evidence Supporting Monell Theories
- Incident report with no articulation of how deputy observed the violation
- Two different vehicle descriptions in the same report (printed 6/20/2023)
- Notarized probable cause document with BLANK judicial review section
- Bodycam footage withheld despite open records request and payment
